How Bonus Tax Works in Ontario

When your Ontario employer pays you a bonus, they must withhold income tax, CPP contributions, and EI premiums before the money reaches your account. Unlike your regular paycheque, a bonus triggers a different calculation under the CRA T4032 annualization method — your employer combines the bonus with your regular pay, projects that total over the full year, and applies the resulting marginal tax rate to the entire bonus in one pay period. That stacking of federal tax, Ontario tax, CPP, and EI is why a $10,000 bonus might deposit only around $6,300.

Importantly, this withholding is a prepayment — not your final tax bill. When you file your T1 return, your actual tax is calculated on total annual income after credits and deductions. If your employer withheld more than you owe, the CRA refunds the difference. CPP and EI on bonuses are also subject to annual maximums — if your year-to-date salary already exceeds those ceilings, no further deductions apply.
